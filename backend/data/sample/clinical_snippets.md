# Sample Clinical Knowledge Base (Demo)

## Cardiology
- STEMI: ST-elevation myocardial infarction requires immediate reperfusion.
- ACS symptoms: chest pain, diaphoresis, dyspnea, radiation to arm/jaw.
- Heart failure: evaluate EF, volume status, guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT).
- Atrial fibrillation: rate vs rhythm control; anticoagulation based on CHA2DS2-VASc.

## Dermatology
- Melanoma ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter >6mm, Evolving.
- Cellulitis vs erysipelas: deeper dermal/subcutis vs superficial; systemic signs guide antibiotics.
- Psoriasis: well-demarcated plaques with silvery scale; consider topical steroids / biologics.
- SJS/TEN: severe cutaneous adverse reaction often 4–28 days after high-risk drugs (carbamazepine, allopurinol, lamotrigine, sulfa); painful rash, mucosal involvement (oral/ocular/genital), fever; stop culprit drug; supportive/burn-unit care; SCORTEN for TEN severity.
- DRESS: drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms — delayed (2–8 weeks) rash, facial edema, lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia, organ involvement (liver, kidney); stop culprit; systemic steroids often considered.
- AGEP: acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis — rapid pustular eruption usually <5 days after antibiotics; fever common; withdraw drug; usually self-limited with supportive care.
- Carbamazepine and aromatic anticonvulsants: well-known association with SJS/TEN and DRESS; HLA-B*1502 risk in some Asian populations for carbamazepine SJS/TEN.

## Neurology
- Stroke FAST: Face droop, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency.
- Migraine vs secondary headache: red flags include thunderclap, fever, neuro deficit, age >50 new onset.
- Seizure first aid: protect airway, time the event, do not put objects in mouth.

## General Internal Medicine
- Sepsis: suspect infection + organ dysfunction; early fluids, cultures, antibiotics.
- Hyponatremia workup: serum osmolality, volume status, urine Na/osmolality.
- Diabetes type 2: metformin first-line if tolerated; screen for complications.

## Clinical Pharmacology & Drug Safety
- Warfarin + NSAIDs: increased bleeding risk.
- ACE inhibitors + potassium-sparing diuretics: hyperkalemia risk.
- QT-prolonging drugs: macrolides, fluoroquinolones, some antipsychotics — check interactions.
- Renal dosing: adjust metformin, DOACs, many antibiotics for reduced eGFR.
- Penicillin allergy: clarify reaction type; many reported allergies are not IgE-mediated.
- Digoxin toxicity: narrow therapeutic index; symptoms include nausea, vomiting, visual changes (xanthopsia), bradycardia, ventricular arrhythmias; check digoxin level, K+, Mg2+; hold digoxin; consider digoxin-specific Fab for life-threatening toxicity.
- Digoxin + clarithromycin (and other macrolides): major interaction via P-glycoprotein inhibition → increased digoxin exposure and toxicity risk; avoid combination or reduce digoxin and monitor levels closely.
- Digoxin interacting drugs: amiodarone, verapamil, diltiazem, quinidine, cyclosporine, azole antifungals also raise digoxin levels — review all new prescriptions for P-gp/CYP interactions.
- Major drug-drug interaction management: identify mechanism, stop or adjust culprit, monitor drug levels and electrolytes, document ADR, deprescribe when risk outweighs benefit.
- Serotonin syndrome: excess serotonergic activity (e.g. SSRI/SNRI such as sertraline combined with tramadol, MAOIs, linezolid, triptans); features include agitation, clonus, hyperreflexia, diaphoresis, fever, tachycardia; stop serotonergic agents; benzodiazepines; cyproheptadine; supportive care/cooling — this is a toxicology/drug-safety emergency, not an ACS workup.
- Sertraline + tramadol: major interaction risk for serotonin syndrome; avoid combination or monitor closely and educate on early toxicity signs.
- Lithium toxicity: narrow therapeutic index; features include vomiting, diarrhea, tremor, ataxia, confusion, nystagmus; risk rises with AKI/dehydration/NSAIDs/ACE inhibitors; hold lithium, check level and renal function, IV fluids, hemodialysis for severe toxicity — clinical pharmacology / toxicology ownership, not ACS.
- Warfarin + ciprofloxacin (and other fluoroquinolones/macrolides/azoles): major interaction raising INR and bleeding risk (epistaxis, bruising, GI bleed); hold or reduce warfarin, check INR, vitamin K if indicated — clinical pharmacology / drug safety.
